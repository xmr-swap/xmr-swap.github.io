What is XMR

What an XMR swap is

An XMR swap exchanges Monero (ticker XMR) for another coin such as USDT, BTC, ETH or LTC, or the other way round. The two assets live on different blockchains, so the trade is handled by an instant-exchange service rather than by a single smart contract.

The XMR ticker

XMR is the coin of the Monero network, a privacy-focused proof-of-work chain. Amounts, senders and recipients are hidden on-chain by default, which is why Monero wallets work differently from Bitcoin or Ethereum wallets.

Supported directions

  • XMR → USDT, BTC, ETH or LTC: you send XMR and receive the other coin.
  • USDT, BTC, ETH or LTC → XMR: you send the other coin and receive XMR.

Each route is quoted by the exchange service behind it. The page shows the route and the amount you are expected to receive before you send anything.

Fixed vs floating

Fixed-rate vs floating-rate quotes

Most XMR routes offer two quote types. The choice decides whether the amount you receive can change between your deposit and the payout.

Fixed-rate vs floating-rate quote
Fixed rateFloating rate
Receive amountSet when the order is createdSet when the deposit is processed
Price movementService carries the riskYou carry the risk
Deposit amountMust match the orderFlexible within the minimum
CostMargin built into the rateUsually a narrower margin

Which to pick

A fixed rate suits an exact target, for example a known amount of XMR. A floating rate suits a send where the exact amount matters less, or where you may deposit a slightly different amount than planned.

How to swap

How to swap XMR step by step

Addresses you will need

Monero has no browser-wallet connect or signature step. Instead you paste a receive address, then send the deposit from your own wallet. For XMR payouts, use a fresh subaddress from your Monero wallet; it keeps incoming swaps separate from your main address.

Step by step

  1. Choose the pair, direction and quote type, then enter the amount. Check it against the minimum amount shown for that route.
  2. Paste your receive address for the coin you are buying, and a refund address for the coin you are sending.
  3. Review the quote and create the order. You get a one-time deposit address and the exact coin and network to use.
  4. Send the deposit from your own wallet to that address, and keep the order ID.
  5. Follow the status: waiting for deposit → confirming → exchanging → sending. The payout arrives at your receive address.

Confirmations on both chains

The deposit must reach the confirmation count the service requires before the exchange starts, and the payout must confirm on its own chain. Monero outputs are also locked for 10 blocks before they can be spent, so XMR can look pending in a wallet for a while.

Fees

What an XMR swap costs

Built into the quote

Instant-exchange services usually earn through a margin on the rate and sometimes a service fee. Both are reflected in the amount you are quoted, so compare the final receive amount, not just the headline rate.

Network fees

Your deposit pays the sending chain's own network fee from your wallet. The payout fee for the receiving chain is normally already deducted from the quoted amount.

Minimum amounts

Each route has a minimum amount, set because small deposits can cost more in network fees than they are worth. A deposit below the minimum may not be exchanged automatically and may need a refund or support request.

Security

Staying safe with XMR swaps

Non-custodial interface

This interface only routes and quotes. It never holds your private keys, and you receive coins at addresses from your own wallet. The exchange service holds your deposit only while the order is processed.

Before you send

  • Copy the deposit address from the order page and verify the coin and network, for example USDT on the correct chain.
  • Enter a refund address you control, so a failed or expired order returns to you.
  • Use a subaddress for XMR you receive, and double-check the first and last characters of every address.
  • Send only once per deposit address, and never reuse an old order's address.
  • Follow the laws and reporting rules that apply to you. Swaps are not tax or legal advice.

FAQ

XMR Swap FAQ

What does an XMR swap do?

An XMR swap trades Monero for USDT, BTC, ETH or LTC, or buys XMR with one of those coins. An instant-exchange service handles both chains and pays out to the address you provide.

Which receive address should I use for XMR?

Use a fresh subaddress from your own Monero wallet. It works like a normal address for the sender but keeps this payout separate from your other incoming funds.

Why do I need a refund address?

If an order expires, is underpaid or cannot be completed, the service needs somewhere to return your deposit. Use an address in your own wallet for the coin you are sending, not an exchange deposit address.

What happens if I send less than the minimum?

Each route lists a minimum amount, and a deposit below it may not be exchanged automatically. It can end up waiting for a refund or a support request, so check the minimum before you send.

Why is my XMR swap still confirming?

The service waits for the deposit to reach its required confirmation count on the sending chain. The payout then needs its own confirmations, and Monero outputs stay locked for 10 blocks before they can be spent.

Should I choose a fixed or floating rate for XMR?

A fixed rate locks the receive amount at order creation, while a floating rate is set when the deposit is processed and can move with the market. Pick fixed when you need an exact amount and floating when flexibility matters more.
